1  Phy-Phox: Physics Phone Experiments

1.1 Introduction

phyphox (pronounced “fee-focks”) stands for Physical Phone Experiments. It is a free mobile app designed to transform your smartphone into a powerful physics laboratory. Developed by researchers at RWTH Aachen University in Germany, phyphox leverages the multitude of sensors embedded in modern smartphones—such as accelerometers, gyroscopes, magnetometers, barometers, microphones, and cameras—to perform real-time physics experiments.

Since its launch, phyphox has become an invaluable tool for educators, students, and enthusiasts around the world, enabling hands-on learning that is accessible, versatile, and engaging.

1.2 Why phyphox?

Smartphones are ubiquitous, and their built-in sensors are sophisticated enough to measure physical phenomena with surprising accuracy. phyphox harnesses these capabilities by providing easy-to-use experimental setups that require minimal additional equipment. This democratizes physics experimentation by removing traditional barriers like expensive lab gear or dedicated sensors.

Key advantages include:

  • Accessibility: Available for free on both Android and iOS.
  • Portability: Experiments can be done anywhere—classrooms, outdoors, or at home.
  • Interactivity: Real-time data visualization and analysis within the app.
  • Flexibility: Customizable experiments and exportable raw data for further study.

1.3 Core Features

1.3.1 1. Sensor-Based Experiments

phyphox offers dozens of predefined experiments using smartphone sensors, including:

  • Accelerometer: Measure acceleration, free fall, pendulum motion, or vibrations.
  • Gyroscope: Analyze rotational motion and angular velocity.
  • Magnetometer: Detect magnetic fields, measure Earth’s magnetic flux density.
  • Barometer: Investigate pressure changes, altitude, and weather-related phenomena.
  • Microphone: Study sound waves, frequency analysis, and resonance.
  • Light Sensor & Camera: Explore optics, light intensity, and color analysis.
  • Proximity Sensor: Time-of-flight measurements and simple motion detection.

1.3.2 2. Real-Time Data Display and Processing

  • Live graphs and numerical data display.
  • Basic data analysis functions like integration, differentiation, and Fourier transform.
  • Adjustable sampling rates and data smoothing.
  • Customizable experiment parameters.

1.3.3 3. Data Export and Sharing

phyphox allows exporting data in standard formats such as CSV, facilitating deeper analysis using spreadsheet software or scientific tools like Python, MATLAB, or Excel.

1.3.4 4. Custom Experiments

Users with technical skills can create or modify experiments using the phyphox experiment file format—a simple XML-based configuration that defines sensors, data processing, and user interface elements. This flexibility supports educators and researchers in tailoring experiments to specific learning goals.

1.4 Example Experiments

Here are some common experiments you can perform with phyphox:

  • Simple Pendulum: Using the accelerometer, measure period and damping.

  • Free Fall: Drop the phone safely and verify acceleration due to gravity.

  • Roller Coaster Analysis: Record acceleration and forces experienced during rides.

  • Sound Frequency Analysis: Capture and analyze sound waves and resonance frequencies.

  • Magnetic Field Mapping: Map spatial variations in magnetic fields using the magnetometer.

  • Barometric Altitude: Track altitude changes when climbing stairs or hills.

  • Rotational Dynamics: Study spinning motions and angular velocities.

1.5 Applications in Education

phyphox fits perfectly into physics education at many levels:

  • Hands-On Learning: Students perform real experiments rather than only simulations or theory.
  • Inquiry-Based Teaching: Encourages hypothesis, measurement, data analysis, and interpretation.
  • Cost-Effective Labs: Removes the need for expensive dedicated sensors.
  • Remote or Hybrid Learning: Supports experimentation outside of traditional classrooms.
  • Cross-Disciplinary Connections: Links physics to technology, engineering, and data science.

Teachers can integrate phyphox into their lesson plans easily. For example, when teaching kinematics, students can measure acceleration during a walk or in a car; when discussing waves, they can analyze sound frequencies or light intensities.

1.6 Technical Requirements and Availability

  • Supported Platforms: Android (Google Play Store) and iOS (Apple App Store).
  • Device Requirements: Modern smartphones with the relevant sensors (most devices made in the last 5–7 years).
  • Cost: Free with no ads or in-app purchases.
  • Languages: Available in multiple languages, including English, German, Spanish, and more.

1.7 Getting Started

  1. Download and install phyphox from your device’s app store.
  2. Browse the list of experiments and select one that fits your lesson or curiosity.
  3. Follow the on-screen instructions to conduct the experiment.
  4. Observe the real-time data visualization.
  5. Export data for further analysis if desired.
  6. Experiment with adjusting parameters or try creating custom experiments.

1.8 Community and Resources

phyphox has an active user and developer community. The official website provides:

  • A comprehensive list of experiments.
  • Tutorials and lesson ideas.
  • Tools to create and share custom experiments.
  • FAQs and support forums.

Educators and students share their experiences and innovative uses online, helping the app grow as a versatile educational resource.
